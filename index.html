Youth Girls Soccer
in America,
from the Sideline up.

More than a million American girls play organized soccer. Behind that number is a sprawling, occasionally bewildering ecosystem of rec leagues, travel clubs, national tournaments, college recruiters, and competing acronyms. This is the guide we wish someone had handed us at the first practice.

For parents, players & coaches 10 chapters Reading time ~ 75 minutes

There has never been a better — or more confusing — time to be a girl who loves soccer in the United States. The sport is bigger, more competitive, and more professionalized than at any point in its history. Pathways that didn't exist a decade ago are now the default. Costs have climbed. Recruiting has gotten earlier and stranger. The medicine has gotten smarter. And the women's national team has, against the odds, made the future feel obvious.

This site is a primer, a translator, and a checklist. It's organized by the questions families actually ask, in roughly the order they ask them: What level should she play at? What does it cost? Will she get hurt? What about college? You can read it cover-to-cover, or jump straight to whatever's keeping you up tonight.

How to use this guide

This isn't a textbook. We organized it the way the questions actually arrive: in waves, often at inconvenient hours, frequently from the back seat of a minivan on the way home from practice.

  • If you're new to soccer itself: open The Game, Explained first. Twenty minutes there will save you weeks of confusion.
  • If your daughter is just starting (U6–U10): start with Pathways to understand what level even is, then read the Parent's Field Manual.
  • If she's leveling up (U11–U14): you want Pathways, Costs, and the ACL section in Health & Safety. This is the age band where injuries spike.
  • If she's thinking about college (U15–U19): read The College Recruiting Roadmap first, then revisit Costs for the realistic financial picture.
  • If you coach: The Coach's Compass is for you, but Health & Safety is the chapter we'd ask every coach to read before the first practice.
