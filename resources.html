Glossary & Resources

Every acronym decoded, the calendar of an American youth soccer year, and a curated list of the organizations, tools, and references we'd hand a family on day one.

The glossary

An opinionated working dictionary of the terms you'll meet in the parking lot, on team apps, and in coach emails. Asterisks mark abbreviations whose specific structures change year to year; check the official source.

U.S. Soccer
The United States Soccer Federation — the national governing body. Sets technical standards and runs national teams.
USYS
U.S. Youth Soccer — the largest youth-soccer body in the country, organized through state associations. Runs the National League and a tiered competition structure.
State association
State-level affiliate of USYS or U.S. Club Soccer (e.g., Eastern New York Youth Soccer, Cal North). Owns rec and travel-league sanctioning, ODP, and registrar functions.
U.S. Club Soccer
An alternative national member organization of U.S. Soccer. Sanctions many competitive clubs; runs ENPL and was historically tied to the Development Academy.
ECNL
Elite Clubs National League. Founded 2009; the most established elite-tier girls' league. U13–U19. See Pathways.
ECRL
ECNL Regional League. The development tier under ECNL within ECNL clubs.
GA
Girls Academy. Launched 2020 as a club-led, player-first national league. Comparable elite tier.
GA Aspire
The Girls Academy's official second tier, launched February 2025 and operated in collaboration with the Development Player League (DPL). U13–U19 regional play, regional and national showcases, and a national championship at each age group.
DPL
Development Player League. Long-running national girls' platform; operates GA Aspire on behalf of the Girls Academy and continues its own DPL and DPL Open competitions beneath it.
MLS Next
Major League Soccer's youth competition. As of 2025–26, boys-only; there is no MLS Next girls' league. In December 2024, MLS Next announced a strategic alliance with the Girls Academy covering coaching education, joint showcases, and integration of select GA clubs into the Generation adidas Cup.
GA / MLS Next Alliance
The Dec 2024 strategic partnership between the Girls Academy and MLS Next. Often misreported in casual conversation as "MLS Next Girls."
NL / NLC / NLP*
USYS National League and its tiers — National League, NL Conferences, NL P.R.O. Structures evolve; see USYS for current configuration.
NPL
National Premier League — a U.S. Club Soccer competition with regional divisions.
EDP
Eastern Development Program — a large regional league across the eastern U.S.
ODP
Olympic Development Program. State and regional ID program run by USYS. Predates the elite leagues.
YNT
Youth National Team. U.S. Soccer's invite-only player pool from roughly U14 to U23.
NCAA
National Collegiate Athletic Association. Governs the largest portion of U.S. college sports across Divisions I, II, and III.
D1 / D2 / D3
NCAA divisions. D1 and D2 offer athletic scholarships; D3 does not. See Recruiting.
NAIA
National Association of Intercollegiate Athletics. ~200 colleges, often smaller. Athletic scholarships available.
NJCAA / JUCO
National Junior College Athletic Association. Two-year colleges with multiple competitive divisions.
NLI
National Letter of Intent. The binding agreement signed by an athlete to a D1 or D2 program.
NIL
Name, Image, and Likeness. Since 2021, college athletes can be paid for personal-brand activities. Rules continue to evolve.
FAFSA
Free Application for Federal Student Aid — the primary form for U.S. college financial aid.
CSS Profile
Additional financial-aid form used by many private universities for institutional aid.
FIFA 11+
The FIFA injury-prevention warm-up protocol. Reduces ACL and other lower-extremity injuries when run as designed. See Safety.
PEP
Prevent Injury and Enhance Performance — Santa Monica Sports Medicine Foundation neuromuscular program.
RED-S
Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport. The modern framework for low-energy-availability syndromes in athletes.
ACL
Anterior cruciate ligament. The knee ligament most commonly injured in cutting sports; girls' soccer carries a particularly high rate.
SafeSport
The U.S. Center for SafeSport — independent body that handles abuse-prevention training and reporting in Olympic and Paralympic sports.
GotSport
The dominant scheduling/registrar platform used by most U.S. youth associations and tournaments.
TeamSnap / Heja
Team-management apps you'll likely have on your phone for years.
Veo / Trace
Automated camera systems that record full games for film review and recruiting clips.
NCSA / SportsRecruits
Recruiting platform/service providers. Useful for organization; not a substitute for the player's own outreach.
Stay-to-play
Tournament policy that requires teams to book hotels through a designated provider. A real driver of weekend tournament cost.
Showcase
A tournament built primarily for college coaches to scout, with games scheduled around scouting visibility rather than competitive draws.
ID camp
A college program's identification camp — typically a 1–2 day event where the program's coaching staff sees recruits in person.
Verbal commitment
A non-binding agreement between a player and a college program that the player intends to attend and play. Becomes binding only at signing.
Transfer portal
The NCAA's database of players who have notified their school of intent to transfer. Has reshaped college roster construction since 2019.
PHV
Peak Height Velocity — the period of fastest growth during adolescence. A meaningful injury-risk window.
ECNL Champions League
The end-of-season national playoff for ECNL teams.
Letter of Intent (NLI) signing periods
Early signing period in November of senior year; regular signing window from April through August.

A typical youth soccer year

What an American club calendar looks like, in broad strokes. Specific seasons vary by region — Spring soccer dominates parts of the Northeast; Fall is the high-school season in much of the country.

WindowWhat's happening
AugustPreseason. Tryouts wrapped, rosters set, league games begin in many regions. Heat is the safety story.
September–OctoberFall league season. Most weekends are league games; one or two tournaments mixed in. High-school season runs in parallel in most states.
NovemberLeague playoffs and regional tournaments. NCAA Early Signing Period for senior commitments.
December–FebruaryIndoor / futsal / training block. ECNL and GA showcase events fall in this window in some regions. ID camps cluster around college off-season.
March–AprilSpring league season opens. State-cup play begins. Recruiting outreach intensifies for U16+ players.
May–JuneState cups conclude. National league playoffs (ECNL Champions League and National Playoff, GA Playoffs). College ID camps peak. June 15 = the recruiting opening bell after sophomore year.
June–JulyNational finals (ECNL National Championships, etc.). Tryouts for the next club year. Off-season for many programs follows.

A short reading list

Books and writers we hand new coaches and curious parents.

  • The Talent Code, Daniel Coyle — accessible introduction to skill acquisition.
  • Inverting the Pyramid, Jonathan Wilson — the history of soccer tactics.
  • How Soccer Explains the World, Franklin Foer — for the cultural perspective.
  • Range, David Epstein — the case against early specialization.
  • The Coaching Habit, Michael Bungay Stanier — short, useful, applicable to coaching humans of any age.
  • Roar, Stacy T. Sims, PhD — sports science specifically for female athletes.

That's the guide. If something is wrong, missing, or out of date — let us know via your favorite channel; we keep this updated.
